# Mediterranean Coded Tapestry

A reusable visual style reference distilled from the four supplied images. This describes the illustrated hero artwork only—not the surrounding website interface, typography, buttons, logos, or copy.

## Style fingerprint

Classical Mediterranean landscapes and architecture rendered like an antique woven tapestry translated through a primitive digital code. Every surface is built from thousands of tiny, irregular loop, dash, zero, and stitch-like marks arranged in loose rows. From a distance the image reads as a serene scenic illustration; up close it becomes a dense field of hand-made symbols.

The result should feel simultaneously:

- ancient and computational
- pastoral and monumental
- tactile and screen-like
- sun-faded but color-rich
- highly detailed without becoming photoreal

Useful shorthand: **“sun-bleached Mediterranean scenery rendered as a coded jacquard tapestry with engraved contours.”**

## Non-negotiable visual traits

1. **All-over micro-mark texture**  
   Cover sky, water, stone, vegetation, figures, and shadows with small looped or dash-like marks. The marks are visible and imperfect, not a smooth digital halftone.

2. **Limited, region-based color**  
   Use roughly 5–8 principal inks. Large areas have distinct color families—blue water, pale blue sky, ochre stone, near-black foliage—while tiny marks create local variation.

3. **Classical Mediterranean subject language**  
   Favor colonnades, arches, villas, temples, statues, cypress and umbrella pine trees, distant mountains, open sea, fields, and sculptural human figures.

4. **Flattened scenic depth**  
   Compose in broad horizontal layers or as a stage-like architectural view. Depth comes from overlapping silhouettes, scale, and color bands rather than realistic atmospheric rendering.

5. **Engraved silhouette clarity**  
   Forms must remain legible beneath the texture. Use crisp contours, simplified shapes, and strong value separation.

6. **Aged print character**  
   Include slight ink irregularity, worn registration, uneven density, and muted paper influence. Keep it refined—not dirty, torn, distressed, or grungy.

## Texture recipe

- Base construction: woven jacquard, needlepoint, or tapestry-like raster
- Mark vocabulary: tiny zeros, ovals, short hooks, dashes, seed stitches, and broken hatch marks
- Mark behavior: hand-drawn variation in size, spacing, tilt, and ink density
- Direction: mostly organized in loose horizontal and vertical rows, curving subtly around forms
- Shading: clusters of denser dark marks rather than smooth gradients
- Highlights: exposed parchment or cream-colored marks
- Edges: articulated with short engraved strokes, never glossy vector outlines
- Texture scale: clearly visible at normal viewing size, while still small enough to merge optically from a distance

Avoid standard Ben-Day dots, clean pixel art squares, uniform cross-stitch Xs, photographic grain, thick impasto, or soft watercolor washes.

## Composition

### Preferred formats

- Wide cinematic banner: 16:7 to 21:9
- Editorial landscape: 3:2
- Square variation: 1:1 with a single statue, arch, or tree as the anchor

### Scene structure

- One dominant anchor: statue, villa, temple, archway, cypress cluster, or mountain
- Broad quiet areas around the anchor
- Strong horizontal bands: foreground / architecture or figure / water or field / mountain / sky
- Optional dark framing mass on one edge to create contrast
- Architectural openings may frame bright blue sea and sky
- Subjects are usually centered or placed near a third, not scattered evenly

### Camera and perspective

- Eye-level or slightly elevated viewpoint
- Frontal, calm, tableau-like staging
- Moderate-to-long view; avoid extreme lens distortion
- Symmetry may be used for arches and colonnades, but landscapes can remain gently asymmetrical

## Palette system

Use a restrained, screen-printed palette. The exact values may shift, but keep the relationships.

### Core inks

| Role | Suggested range | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paper / highlight | warm ivory to pale sand | `#F0D8B4` |
| Sunlit stone | parchment, peach, muted terracotta | `#CDB294`, `#BB9B71` |
| Golden detail | antique ochre | `#B1946A` |
| Sky | faded cornflower or dusty blue | `#95A2AB`, `#778DA3` |
| Water / deep distance | Mediterranean cobalt or petrol blue | `#295275`, `#3A5B6E` |
| Vegetation | muted sage, olive, deep bottle green | `#A3B38C`, `#617159` |
| Structural dark | espresso, charcoal brown, near-black | `#463923`, `#3A2F1E` |
| Optional accent | dusty lavender or faded coral | `#ACB2DF`, `#EDD7C8` |

### Palette modes

- **Sunlit pastoral:** ivory + peach + lavender blue + sage + olive
- **Blue coast:** dusty sky blue + deep cobalt + parchment + espresso + antique gold
- **Shadowed arcade:** near-black brown + ochre + cream + cool blue
- **Ruined temple:** sand + terracotta + slate blue + deep green + parchment

Do not use neon hues, glossy black, pure digital white, or broad rainbow color.

## Light and mood

- Clear Mediterranean daylight or warm late-afternoon sun
- High but softened contrast
- Long, readable shadow masses
- Luminous sky and water balanced by dark trees or architecture
- Mood: contemplative, cultivated, timeless, quietly uncanny
- No cinematic lens flare, volumetric god rays, glossy reflections, or photorealistic depth of field

## Subject guidance

Good subjects:

- a marble bust overlooking the sea
- an empty arcade with three arches
- a hillside villa among cypress trees
- a temple colonnade framing distant mountains
- terraced fields beneath a lavender sunset
- a lone traveler beside an ancient fountain
- a coastal garden with umbrella pines
- an observatory, library, or futuristic device treated like a classical monument

Modern subjects can work if they are simplified into monumental silhouettes and placed inside the same Mediterranean visual world. Let the ancient setting dominate; keep technology understated and sculptural.

## Adjustable prompt controls

Use these phrases to steer variations without losing the style:

- **More textile:** “raised thread impression, stronger woven rows, denser stitch variation”
- **More engraved:** “finer contour hatching, sharper architectural edges, etched shadow clusters”
- **More digital:** “micro-marks resemble tiny zeros and primitive coded glyphs, still hand-made and irregular”
- **More pastoral:** “open fields, pale sky, sage greens, soft lavender distance, generous negative space”
- **More dramatic:** “dark architectural frame, bright cobalt opening, antique gold highlights, deep espresso shadow”
- **More minimal:** “single monumental subject, broad quiet color fields, only five inks”
- **More aged:** “slightly faded ink and imperfect registration”—do not add tears or heavy distress

## Consistency checklist

Before accepting an image, verify:

- The micro-marks cover nearly every surface and remain visibly irregular.
- The image reads clearly both as a whole and at texture level.
- The palette is limited and separated into broad regional color masses.
- The scene has one unmistakable visual anchor.
- Depth is flattened and scenic rather than photographic.
- Stone, sky, water, and vegetation share the same mark-making system.
- The image feels aged and tactile, but not damaged or dirty.
- No website interface, typography, logo, or button has leaked into the artwork.
